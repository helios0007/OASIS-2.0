How OASIS works now, and the full tool it is the first part of

OASIS predicts how many hours of a recorded Chennai heatwave week a home without AC stays safe for the people in it, with a daily power cut that stops the ceiling fans, and which design changes add safe hours. The first pipeline is the tool as it runs today (the MVP). The second is the complete thesis tool, with each part marked built, partly built or planned.

Pipeline 1

The tool as it runs today

Three things are prepared once and reused by every run: the weather, the calibrated physics and the AI estimate. After that, each assessment goes through the same seven steps.

Prepared once

A · Weather of a real heatwave

Hourly records from the Meenambakkam airport station plus ERA5 sunlight. The week is the hottest of April–June 2016–2025 by outdoor heat index: 1–7 May 2024. Two more outdoor-air cases are made from it: city centre (measured Nungambakkam offsets) and street with AC waste heat (+1 °C at night, from literature).

get_heatwave.py · check_city_weather.py · oasis/ac_heat.py · oasis/scenario.py
Givesthree weather files: airport, city centre, street AC

B · Physics fitted to measured homes

One model per monitored Dhaka home, built from its survey (Tasgaonkar et al. 2022). 1,440 runs fix what no survey or standard gives: internal mass 320 m², appliances 100 W, wall leakage 8 cm²/m², air moved by the AirflowNetwork. Checked on Delhi homes without refitting.

calibrate_homes.py --airflow afn · check_evening.py · check_lag.py
Givesthe default settings in oasis/build_idf.py

C · Sweeps and the AI estimate

Every combination of the design choices is simulated for each outdoor-air case (3 × 1,512 runs), plus 1,500 random rooms of other sizes and orientations. XGBoost learns safe hours and heat dose from them. Error on designs it has not seen: 1.5 h on the grid, 5.2 h on random rooms.

run_sweep.py · run_sample.py · train_surrogate.py · oasis/surrogate.py
Givesstored results for the static tool, and an instant estimate
▼ used by every run ▼
Every run
  1. 1

    Describe the home, the people and the scenario

    • Home, one of three ways: typed choices (size, orientation, floor, wall, roof, roof colour, window share, overhang, windows at night); a Rhino .3dm of one room; or a room-by-room plan drawn on OASIS:: layers (the two Amudha Elite flats are bundled).
    • People: each person's age, sex and daily routine by room.
    • Scenario: outdoor air (airport / city centre / street AC) and the power cut (start hour, length 1 / 4 / 10 / 24 h, or none).

    While the design is changed but not yet run, the web tool shows the AI estimate for a single room.

    frontend/ · app.py · oasis/from_rhino.py · oasis/read_layout_3dm.py · /api/predict
    Hands onone set of design parameters, or a plan with rooms, walls, doors and openings
  2. 2

    Write the EnergyPlus models

    The model file is written from scratch in Python, with no template. A single room is one zone; a plan gets one zone per room, real partitions and doors, and shared walls with no sun or wind. The design is written once as drawn and once for every single change that can be made to it (8 models for the baseline room). Files are written one after another because the writer library is not thread-safe.

    oasis/build_idf.py · oasis/build_layout.py · oasis/assess.py:single_changes
    Hands ona set of .idf models that differ by one choice each
  3. 3

    Simulate the heatwave

    EnergyPlus 26.2 runs the models side by side, up to 8 at a time. Each run starts on 24 April so the walls and roof are already warm, and the seven spin-up days are dropped. Any severe error in the EnergyPlus log stops the run.

    oasis/simulate.py · config.py
    Hands on168 hourly rows per model: indoor air and radiant temperature, humidity, outdoor air
  4. 4

    Turn temperatures into felt heat and safe hours

    • Heat index (US NWS) every hour from indoor air temperature and humidity.
    • Ceiling fan: the ASHRAE 55 cooling effect, about 2 °C, is taken off every hour, except in the power-cut hours.
    • Safe hours: hours of the week with felt heat index below 41 °C. This is the headline.
    • Heat dose beside it: felt heat above 27 °C summed over the week, against 850 °C·h (elderly) and 1,174 °C·h (young adults) from Chen et al. 2024.
    • Cross-check with the extended heat index (Lu & Romps 2022), and the two peaks: the daily one and the one during the cut.

    A plan is scored where the people are: the living room by day, the hottest bedroom at night.

    oasis/metrics.py · oasis/unit.py
    Hands onsafe hours, cut hours in danger, peak heat index and heat dose for every model
  5. 5

    Rank the changes, build packages, price them

    Each single change is ranked by safe hours gained over the design as drawn. Packages are then built step by step: start from the best change, simulate it with each remaining change added, keep the one that reaches most safe hours, up to three. Packages are always simulated, never added up, because changes overlap. Each change and package gets a cost: rate × the home's own roof, wall and window areas, minus the choice it replaces.

    oasis/assess.py · oasis/costs.py
    Hands onranked changes and packages with safe hours gained, ₹, and hours per ₹1,000
  6. 6

    Check the result on bodies

    Each adult is run hour by hour through the JOS-3 body model with the room's air, radiant temperature and humidity. Core temperature below 38 °C counts as safe. The tool reports how many hours the heat index and the bodies agree. Children have no validated body model and are covered by the heat index only.

    oasis/agents.py
    Hands oncore and skin temperature per person per hour, and the agreement with the heat index
  7. 7

    Show it

    • Web tool (live): 3D view of the home, week timeline, and four tabs: Summary, Interventions, People, Basis. Basis lists every input as measured, calibrated, standard or chosen.
    • Streamlit app (live): the same run with a simpler layout.
    • Static page (no EnergyPlus): looks the design up in the stored sweeps.
    • Command line: one Rhino file in, safe hours and ranked changes out.
    run_server.py + backend/main.py + frontend/ · app.py · make_tool.py · run_rhino.py
    Hands onan answer in about 20–30 seconds for a live run
Pipeline 2

The full tool, and how much of it the MVP covers

The complete tool has two layers. The city layer says where heat risk is highest and passes the local conditions down. The home layer, which is this thesis, says how long a home stays safe and what fixes it. Inputs flow through nine process steps to the outputs.

Layer 0 · neighbourhood and city First version

HVRA 1.0: heat vulnerability per building, urban heat island, neighbour shading, street microclimate. It exists for Barcelona. For Chennai, a first Neighbourhood tab reads satellite building heights (Google Open Buildings 2.5D) and OpenStreetMap outlines, runs the Urban Weather Generator to get street air, and passes neighbour shading to the single-room model. Wind is worked out but not passed on; the vulnerability score is not built.

Passes downlocal air temperature, shading, wind and a vulnerability score per building

Layer 1 · building, flat and room MVP running

OASIS: safe hours in a heatwave with a power cut, and the design changes that add them. Today it stands on measured airport and city-centre weather in place of Layer 0.

Inputswhat the user or the tool supplies

ALocation and climatePartlyChennai only, recorded heatwave, three outdoor-air cases. No map picker, no future climate.
BHome geometryPartlyTyped choices, Rhino room, room-by-room plan. No IFC, no plan image or DXF.
CEnvelope and materialsPartlyThree walls, four roof build-ups, roof colour, overhang. One glass type, no defaults by construction year.
DPlace in the buildingPartlyTop or middle floor; shared walls in plans. No corridors, shafts or courtyards.
EUrban contextPartlyStreet air temperature from the Neighbourhood tab (modelled, no sea breeze), beside the measured city-centre case; neighbour shading from satellite building heights (single room only). No wind or vulnerability score.
FOccupantsPartlyAge, sex and routine per person. No health or mobility details; children have no body model.
GSystems and backup powerPartlyCeiling fan only. No AC, coolers, inverter battery, solar or cooking heat.
HGrid and power cutsPartlyA chosen start hour and length, shown beside measured cuts in 18 monitored Chennai homes (Prayas ESMI, 2015–2018). Nothing for the home's own feeder.
IBehaviourPartlyWindows open or shut at night. No curtains, roof wetting or sleeping on the terrace.
JCost and constraintsPartlyCost per change, two published rates and the rest estimates. No budget, tenure or new-versus-existing mode.

Processwhat happens to the inputs

1Intake and defaultsPartlyReads the three geometry inputs and fills the baseline. No lookup by era or climate zone.
2Urban groundingPartlyThe weather file takes street air from the urban model, or measured station offsets. The model ran 0.8 °C too warm at night against the one Chennai station.
3Build the thermal modelBuiltSingle room and multi-room models, calibrated physics.
4ScenarioPartlyRecorded heatwave and a chosen cut. No AC or backup schedules.
5SimulateBuiltEnergyPlus for the result, the AI estimate before a run.
6AssessBuiltSafe hours per room and household, heat dose, body check per adult.
7IntervenePartlyFive kinds of change. The full catalogue of 10–15 measures with feasibility filters is not there.
8Re-simulate and rankBuiltEvery change and package is simulated on the same week and ranked.
9VerifyPartlyFitted on Dhaka, checked on Delhi. Chennai homes are planned for the 2027 field campaign.

Largest gaps between the MVP and the full tool
  1. Chennai measurements. The physics is fitted on Dhaka homes. The 2027 field campaign should log indoor conditions, window opening times and street air beside AC units.
  2. Urban layer. Replace the two stand-in air cases with Layer 0 output for the building's own street.
  3. Systems and backup. AC, inverter batteries and cooking heat, which lead to backup sizing and peak load.
  4. Costs and catalogue. Check the estimate rates against the Tamil Nadu PWD schedule and widen the list of measures.
  5. Regulation and guidance. The code check and the design guidelines are the outputs meant for architects.
